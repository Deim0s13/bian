# Project context and audience

## Primary collaborator

The project is led by an experienced enterprise and pre-sales architect with a
strong cloud-native and platform engineering background. Explanations should
assume familiarity with enterprise architecture, Kubernetes, OpenShift,
cloud-native platforms, governance, and regulated environments.

The useful level of conversation is typically the connection between customer
outcomes, architecture choices, operational consequences, and credible delivery.
Do not default to introductory explanations or detailed coding mechanics.

## Preferred analytical lenses

Work should consider, where relevant:

- customer and user outcomes;
- architecture trade-offs and constraints;
- enterprise and platform operating models;
- governance, security, privacy, and risk;
- integration and data complexity;
- operational resilience and service ownership;
- practical adoption, migration, and support implications;
- enterprise AI uncertainty, evidence, oversight, and lifecycle realities;
- portability across cloud, self-managed, Kubernetes, and OpenShift contexts;
- open-source community, contributor, maintainer, and adopter needs.

## Financial-services context

Banking scenarios should use BIAN language where BIAN defines the relevant
concept. They should also account for regulatory obligations, data governance,
privacy, operational resilience, security, risk management, integration history,
banking capabilities, Service Domain alignment, and operating-model impacts.

BIAN does not define every product, security, regulatory, customer, or technical
concept needed by this project. Non-BIAN concepts must be clearly identified and
must not be presented as BIAN terminology.

## Stakeholder perspectives

Architecture and product work should identify whose concern is being addressed.
The recurring perspectives are:

| Stakeholder | Typical concern |
|---|---|
| CIO | Investment value, portfolio change, risk, and strategic coherence |
| CTO | Architecture fitness, evolution, integration, and technology strategy |
| CISO | Threats, trust boundaries, control effectiveness, and response |
| Enterprise architecture | Capability alignment, traceability, standards, and target state |
| Platform teams | Paved paths, autonomy, operability, and platform sustainability |
| Risk and compliance | Scope, evidence, accountability, limitations, and auditability |
| Application owners | Ownership, impact, migration effort, and service continuity |
| Operations and SRE | Reliability, observability, recovery, capacity, and supportability |
| Developers | Clear contracts, safe change, feedback speed, and usable guidance |
| Open-source maintainers | Reviewability, sustainability, security response, and governance |

Stakeholder viewpoints should not become a generic checklist. Include those that
materially affect the decision being made.

## Validation context

The project will not rely on a participating bank for product trials. Horizon
Synthetic Bank is the fictional institution used to exercise scenarios and
generate repeatable evidence with synthetic information.

Synthetic validation can test behaviour and internal claims. It cannot prove
customer demand, real-bank adoption, regulatory acceptance, or realised business
outcomes. Those limitations must stay visible.
